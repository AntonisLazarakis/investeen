Real headlines, updated automatically every few minutes.

Stocks

US stocks hit a new record high

The S&P 500 reached its first all-time high since August and finished the week up about 1.2%. It's now up around 14% for 2026, with the tech-heavy Nasdaq up about 18%.

Why it matters: A record high doesn't tell you a crash is coming next, or that one isn't. Nobody knows. That's why steady, long-term investing usually beats trying to guess the top.

Interest rates

Borrowing costs hit a 20-year high

The 10-year US Treasury yield climbed to its highest level since 2002. A big government bond sale paid investors 5.3%.

Why it matters: Higher rates make loans more expensive, but savings accounts and bonds pay you more.

The Fed

Another rate hike may be coming

Notes from the Federal Reserve's September meeting confirmed a unanimous quarter-point rate hike. Markets expect one more before December.

Why it matters: Central banks raise rates to slow down rising prices (inflation).

Tech

Worries about AI spending shake tech

Reports that OpenAI's revenue was weaker than expected briefly pulled stocks down, until the company said its forecast hadn't changed.

Why it matters: Companies are spending huge sums on AI, and investors are nervous about whether it will pay off.

Energy

Oil is up nearly 60% this year

A hurricane shut down about 1.3 million barrels a day of production in the Gulf of Mexico, and attacks on ships cut traffic through the Strait of Hormuz.

Why it matters: Oil prices show up in what everyone pays, from plane tickets to groceries.

Chips

Huge growth still disappointed investors

Chipmaker TSMC reported sales up 51%, and Samsung's profit rose nine-fold. Both still fell short of what investors were hoping for.

Why it matters: Stock prices react to results compared with expectations.

Advice that works at any age

General tips to build good habits. Not personal financial advice: talk to a parent or guardian before you invest real money.

  • Start early, even small

    Time does the heavy lifting. Money invested at 15 has decades to grow before money invested at 35 even starts.

  • Build a safety cushion first

    Keep some savings you can reach fast. Only invest money you won't need for a few years.

  • Don't put all your eggs in one basket

    Index funds like the S&P 500 own hundreds of companies, so one bad company can't sink you.

  • Ignore the hype

    If someone promises fast, guaranteed returns, it's a red flag. Real investing is slow and a bit boring.

  • Watch the fees

    A 1% yearly fee sounds tiny, but over 40 years it can eat around a third of your gains.

  • Under 18? Use a custodial account

    In most countries you need a parent or guardian to open an investment account for you until you're 18.

        Words that make the news make sense

        The terms you'll see in our posts, the headlines and the simulator, explained like a friend would.

        Stock (share)
        A tiny piece of a company. Own one Apple share and you own a very small slice of Apple.
        Index fund
        A fund that buys every company in an index. The S&P 500 holds 500 of the biggest US companies at once.
        Sector ETF
        A fund that holds companies from one part of the economy, like energy or health care.
        Bond
        A loan you give to a government or company. They pay you interest and return the money later.
        Dividend
        Cash a company or fund pays its owners, usually every three months.
        Diversification
        Spreading money across many investments so one bad result can't wreck everything.
        Volatility
        How much a price swings. Tesla swings a lot. A bond fund barely moves.
        Earnings
        A company's profit, reported every three months. Prices often jump or drop on earnings day.
        Inflation
        Prices rising over time. If inflation is 3%, something that cost $100 last year costs about $103 now.
        Interest rate
        The price of borrowing money. When rates rise, loans cost more, savings pay more and bond prices fall.
        Market hours
        The US stock market is open 9:30 am to 4 pm New York time on weekdays. That's 4:30 to 11 pm in Greece most of the year.
        Rebalancing
        Buying and selling to get back to the mix you planned after some investments grow faster than others.
